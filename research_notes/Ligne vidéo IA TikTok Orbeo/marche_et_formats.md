## 1. What happened since March 2026: Tentafruit, its spin-offs, Fruit Love Island, other AI series, and which ones kept growing

### Takeaway
Tentafruit (@onlymoviesfr) did not fade out. It turned into a business. Brand integrations (Oasis, Macadam) came first, then in June 2026 TF1+ co-produced a daily AI spin-off ("Skibidi Exfruit", a parody of TF1's own show *Excape Island*) with the same creators. A "season 2" circulates, but on a swarm of lookalike accounts, and no verified current follower count was found. Fruit Love Island stopped for good on 2026-03-28 and was never revived. The AI series that kept growing for 6+ months were not trend-chasers. They are authored universes with lore and a recognizable creator voice: Neural Viz's Monoverse, and outside AI, Chikn Nuggit and Skibidi Toilet.

**Other AI series and characters: who kept growing, and why**

### Inferences
- Tentafruit's lasting value came from **French brand and broadcaster deals**: Oasis, Macadam, then TF1+ in June. It did not come from TikTok payouts or merch. The players with real assets (a broadcaster's format, real contestants' likeness, ad budgets) have now absorbed the reality-parody lane. A newcomer cannot be "first" there any more.
- Neither fruit series built an IP it fully owns: both parody formats owned by others (ITV, TF1/W9). Fruit Love Island died from removals and creator burnout. Tentafruit got co-opted by the owner of a parodied format. This is the case for an **original universe** rather than a parody of a TV format.
- The lookalike-account swarm around Tentafruit, together with "season 2" posts from unofficial accounts, shows the fan-facing brand has been diluted. A durable IP needs a unique, protected name, a single official handle on each platform, and pinned "official account" signposting from day one.
- The accounts that last share four traits: authored lore that spans episodes, a recognizable art direction chosen on purpose (not "default AI"), the creator's own performance or voice, and a cadence fans can count on (Neural Viz near-weekly; Chikn Nuggit 300+ clips; see Q4). Speed-to-trend is not one of them.
- With Following-feed views at 2% and 96% of reach in 10 days, followers on TikTok do not reliably bring repeat viewers. Serial IP must win the For You feed on every episode. Cliffhanger titles and episode numbers matter more than the follower count.

### Gaps
- No current (October 2026) follower count for @onlymoviesfr: TikTok, Social Blade and YouTube were blocked. There is no data on whether its audience held after April, or whether "season 2" was posted by the official account or only by lookalikes.
- No data on what TF1 paid the creators, or on the audience of Skibidi Exfruit after its first 72 hours.
- No verified update on the French clones at 150K–865K followers named in the 2026-10-06 study.

## 2. What works now (Aug–Oct 2026): formats, mechanics, saturation and demographics

### Takeaway
There is far more AI video than ever and fewer views to go around: TikTok views are down 31% year on year while posts are up 80%, and 59% of a new account's For You feed is AI. The formats getting traction now are (a) serial vertical dramas, increasingly made by professionals (TF1, France.tv Slash, ARTE, US microdrama studios), (b) a distinctive recurring character with a fixed look, such as Jean Phil, (c) AI-written but human-delivered comedy, and (d) immersive or nostalgic AI "POV" pieces. Generic fruit, cat and slop soaps are saturated. Hard demographic data for AI series in France does not exist publicly. What there is points to a very young skew (under-25s and children), which is a risk for a teen/adult line.

## 3. Platform attitudes in 2026: labels, "see less AI", removals, and treatment of "unoriginal/mass-produced" content

### Takeaway
All three platforms now aim at **mass-produced, templated or interchangeable** content, judged at the account or channel level. None of them targets AI as such. TikTok says officially that its AI label does not reduce distribution, but viewers can dial AI down in their feed. YouTube's July 2026 clarification explicitly names "characters put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" and "AI clips stitched together without a clear arc" as non-monetizable. That describes most fruit and cat soaps. Instagram ranks originality first. A serial with real story arcs, a stable original cast and visible human authorship fits all three rulebooks.

- Durable AI IP should show human authorship: credited writers or creator, a creator voice or performance (the Neural Viz model), behind-the-scenes posts, and original music and voices. That satisfies Instagram's "originality" framing and TikTok's "original creators" rationale.

## 4. Lasting IP born in short-form (AI or not): what made it durable

### Takeaway
Short-form IP lasts when it has one or more recognizable characters, a consistent visual signature, a large back-catalogue, and a clear rights owner who can license it. Skibidi Toilet (one owner, about 47M subscribers, $25M raised, a Michael Bay-advised film), Chikn Nuggit (TikTok-born, BuzzFeed-backed, merch and a series in development) and Lucas the Spider (YouTube shorts turned into a TV series) all fit. Italian brainrot shows the opposite case: huge reach but no clear owner, so the value went to unlicensed toys, Roblox games and whoever grabbed rights. A French precedent, *Bref.* (episodes under 3 min, then a record Disney+ revival), shows that French short-format comedy can turn into lasting IP.

- *Bref.* shows French audiences will follow a short-format comedy for years when it is built on a strong authorial voice and everyday French life.

2. **Fairy-tale characters in modern French daily life** (job centre, dating apps, the RER, the family dinner). The characters are public domain, so the cast is ownable once redesigned. The comedy register of *Bref.* (everyday-life observation) is a proven French appetite.
3. **Regional France cast** (a Breton, a Marseillais, a Ch'ti, a Parisian as rival objects or animals in a shared setting). This plays on very French identity humour and suits brand tie-ins with regional products. Risk: stereotypes. It needs careful, affectionate writing so it does not repeat Tentafruit's backlash.
4. **Office or school life with a non-human cast** (animals or objects). It hits the 25–34 core and is brand-safe, but it is closer to saturated "animal soap" territory unless the arcs and the art direction stand out strongly.
5. A **silent or near-silent recurring character** (Jean Phil style) as a secondary "feeder" account. It travels beyond French speakers, is cheap to produce daily, and is good for growing the IP between episodes.
- Mechanics to keep from the fruit series:
  - Numbered episodes, a recap host and a "VOUS ÊTES POUR QUI ?" vote in comments (not an off-platform Google Form).
  - A fixed daily slot (19:00 or 18–21h).
  - Original music and voices.
  - Episodes over 60 s for Creator Rewards.
  - Compilations with an arc for YouTube.
- What to drop: parodying a format owned by a broadcaster (ITV threat, TF1 co-option), sexualized or crude humour (mass-report removals, child-audience backlash), and lookalike-vulnerable branding.
