# Kids' brands born on YouTube or in apps that became media franchises: mechanisms, numbers, and the openings left for a small automated studio (Orbeo / "Lulu la Luciole")

Research date: 2026-10-06. Scope: worldwide, plus a section on starting in French. Many trade-press pages (Kidscreen, License Global, Variety, Hollywood Reporter, Billboard, Licensing Letter) returned 402/403 to the fetch tool, so some facts below come from search-result snippets of those pages. Each one is flagged "(snippet)". Data older than 2025 is flagged "(older data)". vidIQ could not be used: the account had 0 credits left (the first call was refused and nothing was charged), so YouTube stats come from public pages and press releases.

---

## 1. Case studies with numbers (subscribers, views, revenue, deals, timelines)

### Takeaway
Every franchise studied went through a long phase of building a content catalog, then reached an "IP moment" when one recurring, recognizable character (or character family) appeared. Monetization through licensing, streaming, film and music followed. The biggest outcomes (CoComelon about $3B, Pinkfong about KRW 545B market cap at IPO, Masha 50,000+ SKUs) went to catalogs of hundreds or thousands of videos built around one simple character and later spread across many languages. Most were then turned into businesses by a specialist owner or acquirer (Moonbug, Animaj, pocket.watch, Fresh TV).

### Cited Findings

**CoComelon (Treasure Studio → Moonbug → Candle Media; Jazwares; Netflix → Disney+; Universal film)**
- The channel was created in September 2006 by Jay Jeon under the username "checkgate". It was renamed "ABC Kid TV" in 2013 and "CoComelon" in 2018, when the recurring characters (JJ and family) were introduced. — [Wikipedia: CoComelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon)
- Subscriber path: 41 (Sept 2007) → 1,287 (Nov 2010) → about 750,000 (Oct 2015) → 1M (May 16, 2016). It added 36M in 2019 to reach 67.4M, passed 100M on Dec 12, 2020, and had 200M subscribers and 220.04B views as of April 2026. Growth took off after the 2018 character rebrand. — [Wikipedia: CoComelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon)
- Netflix launch in June 2020 with three hour-long episodes (compilations). Added to Roku and Hulu in 2020. — [Wikipedia: CoComelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon)
- The toy partnership with Jazwares was announced in February 2020, before the Moonbug acquisition (July 2020). The first line reached retail in early September 2020 with plush, vehicles, figurines and role play, priced from $5 mini vehicles to the $20 "Musical Bedtime JJ Doll" (JJ plush plus a Teddy pillow plush). Jazwares was restocking because of demand. The channel then had 92M subscribers, 3.5B monthly views, and 1B views in a single week (May 2020). — [Tubefilter, 2020-09-02 (older data)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2020/09/02/cocomelon-enters-toy-business-replenishing-stock/); [License Global (snippet)](https://www.licenseglobal.com/toys-games/jazwares-debuts-first-cocomelon-toy-line)
- Moonbug was founded on Feb 9, 2018 in London by René Rechtman (ex-Maker Studios) and John Robson (ex-WildBrain). Acquisitions:
  - Little Baby Bum, Sept 2018, about $9M
  - Morphle, Feb 2019
  - Supa Strikas, Aug 2019
  - Arpo, Feb 2020
  - CoComelon and Blippi, July 2020, alongside a $120M round
  - Little Angel, Feb 10, 2022
  - One Animation (Oddbods), May 2022; closed in Feb 2024
  
  About 30 people were laid off in Dec 2023, when the company refocused on CoComelon and Blippi and said it would explore "artificial intelligence and new technologies while emphasizing music and video games". — [Wikipedia: Moonbug Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonbug_Entertainment)
- Note: Tubefilter describes the CoComelon deal price as undisclosed, following a $120M funding round. The $120M is the round, not necessarily the price. — [Tubefilter (older data)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2020/09/02/cocomelon-enters-toy-business-replenishing-stock/)
- Candle Media (Kevin Mayer and Tom Staggs, backed by Blackstone) bought Moonbug in November 2021 for about $3B in cash and stock. At the time Moonbug was said to be "on track to generate $100 million in profit" that year, expected to double to $200M the next year. This was a forward-looking claim made at the time. — [Yahoo/"Inside the $3 Billion Deal" (older data)](https://www.yahoo.com/now/inside-3-billion-deal-kids-180009057.html)
- 2025: CoComelon grew "from 60 million YouTube subscribers when Candle acquired it… to 200 million today". In January alone, Moonbug properties generated 10B views. Moonbug and Sanrio signed a multi-year partnership: a crossover series with Hello Kitty, Cinnamoroll and other characters joining JJ, debuting in 2026. Moonbug hired Ashley Maidy (ex-Riot Games) as MD of Consumer Products. — [Variety 2025 (snippet)](https://variety.com/2025/tv/news/cocomelon-hello-kitty-moonbug-taylor-swift-1236439880); [Moonbug news](https://moonbug.com/news/cocomelon-and-sanrio-team-up-in-new-global-partnership)
- Netflix did not renew. CoComelon moves to Disney+ from 2027 (all 8 seasons, plus the Little Angel spinoff, which is popular in Latin America), reportedly for "tens of millions annually". Viewership had fallen about 60% in recent years: top 5 across all streaming in 2023, out of the top 10 in 2024. Netflix's narrative spinoff *CoComelon Lane* passed 50M views in H2 2024, more than any season of the main show, and stays on Netflix with Blippi. — [Señal News, 2025-05-26](https://senalnews.com/en/content/disney-will-be-the-new-home-of-cocomelon-in-2027)
- An aggregator reports 273M Netflix viewing hours in H1 2025. This is unverified against Netflix's own engagement report. — [Vitrina (aggregator)](https://vitrina.ai/blog/entertainment-mna-trends/)
- *CoComelon: The Movie* is from Universal and DreamWorks Animation with Moonbug, Flywheel Media and Prime Focus. Release dates conflict between Feb 19 and Feb 26, 2027. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/universal-cocomelon-movie-release-date); [Wikipedia: CoComelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon)
- Live: the "Party Time" touring experience started in June 2023. A Spotify podcast deal and a Dominican Republic resort partnership came in 2022. — [Wikipedia: CoComelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoComelon)
- Music: Moonbug launched **Big Feelings Music** on Sept 22, 2026, a children's music label and publishing/distribution service for third-party creators (Snoop Dogg's Doggyland, Badanamu, Bob Zoom, Lotty Friends, etc.). Children's music was the 9th-largest US on-demand audio genre in 2025 with 15.7B streams. CoComelon's "Wheels on the Bus" was the world's most-streamed music video in 2025 (2B+ video streams; 9B+ lifetime YouTube views). — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/snoop-doggs-doggyland-joins-big-feelings-music-a-new-childrens-music-company-from-cocomelon-owner-moonbug-entertainment)

**Pinkfong / Baby Shark / Bebefinn (The Pinkfong Company, KOSDAQ 2025)**
- Founded in 2010 as SmartStudy with 3 people. It grew to about 100 staff before Baby Shark and has about 340 today, with offices in Seoul, Tokyo, Shanghai and LA. Baby Shark made about 50% of revenue in the years right after release and about 25% now. Post-IPO valuation is over $400M. Other IPs include Bebefinn and Sealook. — [New Straits Times, Nov 2025](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/lifestyle/groove/2025/11/1319269/showbiz-baby-shark-90-second-clip-rm17-billion-business)
- Timeline:
  - Nov 25, 2015: original animated video
  - June 17, 2016: "Baby Shark Dance" (with child performers and dance moves)
  - 2017: goes viral in Indonesia and Southeast Asia; the mobile app passes 150M downloads
  - Aug 2018: reaches the West
  - Jan 7, 2019: Billboard Hot 100 at #32
  - Oct 2019: *Baby Shark Live* stage musical
  - Nov 2, 2020: most-viewed YouTube video ever (7.04B)
  - Dec 2020: Nickelodeon series *Baby Shark's Big Show!*
  - Jan 2022: first video to 10B views
  - Dec 8, 2023: feature film
  - 2024: LG Smart TV app in 184 countries
  
  Wikipedia attributes the virality to "simple repetition" plus easy hand choreography, amplified by K-pop groups (Blackpink, Twice). — [Wikipedia: Baby Shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Shark)
- Licensing ramp (older data): 35 merchandising deals (toys, apparel, home, health and beauty, food, publishing) with WowWee, Hasbro and HarperCollins. Revenue rose 47% in a year, partly from those deals. WowWee Baby Shark products sold out on Amazon in two days at Christmas. There was a 100-date North American tour with Round Room Live, and *Baby Shark's Big Broadwave Tour!* debuted in the US in Feb 2024. — [CNBC 2019 (snippet, older data)](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/06/17/viral-baby-shark-song-to-expand-brand-to-tv-concerts-and-more.html)
- IPO on KOSDAQ, Nov 18, 2025 (ticker 403850): priced at KRW 38,000 and opened at KRW 58,000 (+52.6%). It raised about KRW 76B at a market cap of about KRW 545.3B. Institutional demand was 615.9:1, retail 846.9:1, with about KRW 8T in deposits. Proceeds go to the content pipeline, new IP launches, location-based entertainment venues, and **"OneVoice", an AI-driven localization solution**. — [Pinkfong press release](https://www.thepinkfongcompany.com/en/news/pr/124)
- 2024 revenue KRW 97.4B, operating profit KRW 18.8B. This comes from an aggregator and was not checked against the prospectus. — [Techleap Finder (aggregator)](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/pinkfong-s-kosdaq-ipo-54-53b-valuation)
- IPO preview figures: Baby Shark channel at about 80.5M subscribers (Dec 2024); Baby Shark Dance at about 16.2B views (Aug 2025); about 496M cumulative app downloads across about 20 major apps. CEO Kim Min-seok holds 18.44%, Samsung Publishing 16.77%. — [Douglas Research Insights](https://douglasresearch.substack.com/p/the-pinkfong-company-ipo-preview)

  - **Korean-language content: 11.1B views (+296%), 90% from overseas viewers (up from 75%)**
  - Netflix Kids: #1 in 9 countries, Top 10 in 13
  
  — [Sports Khan, 2026-03-24](https://sports.khan.co.kr/en/article/202603242205007)

This conflicts slightly with the 51.3B figure above and is probably a later date. — [Sports Khan search snippet](https://sports.khan.co.kr/en/article/202604202351007)

A Bebefinn film was released on Netflix in Korea on Dec 24, followed by a global theatrical run and then Netflix worldwide (Apr 2026 headline). — [Licensing Source (snippet)](https://licensingsource.net/brand-alliance-partners-with-pinkfong-on-bebefinn-property); [Sports Khan](https://sports.khan.co.kr/en/article/202512230004047); [Licensing Magazine headline, 2026-04-29](https://www.licensingmagazine.com/2026/04/29/bebefinn-heads-to-netflix-worldwide-following-global-theatrical-run/)

**Pocoyo (Zinkia → Animaj, a French AI-assisted studio)**
- Premiered Jan 7, 2005 on La 2. Episodes run 7 minutes on a plain white 3D background with an off-screen narrator (Stephen Fry in English). Season 4 (2016) was **released exclusively on YouTube**, with 60+ YouTube specials. Season 6 premiered on YouTube June 26, 2026. A third feature film is planned for 2028. Animaj uses "Sketch-to-Motion" AI tools and plans a new series every 12–18 months. — [Wikipedia: Pocoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocoyo)

Its thesis is that the next kids' franchises "will be born on platforms like YouTube, TikTok, Spotify, Netflix, and Roblox". — [Animaj](https://www.animaj.com/post/animaj-fundraising); [Señal News](https://senalnews.com/en/content/animaj-raises-85m-to-scale-ai-driven-global-kids-franchises)

**"AI-made" kids IP**

### Inferences
- **The franchise starts when a character appears, not when the channel starts.** CoComelon was a generic nursery-rhyme channel for 12 years (2006–2018) and only exploded after JJ and family were introduced: +36M subscribers in 2019, toys signed in Feb 2020. Lulu and Nino already have that character core from day one, which is an advantage over channels that brand later.
- **Typical sequence:** catalog (hundreds of videos) → compilations → one consumer product, often plush, sometimes bedtime-themed → streaming licence (Netflix/Disney+) → live show → film. Cash-wise, merch dominates mature brands (Masha 60%). YouTube ads fund the catalog but are rarely the main profit pool once the IP is established.
- **A strong buyer/operator ecosystem exists**: Moonbug, Animaj (French), pocket.watch, Fresh TV, WildBrain. A small studio with a well-defined character and strong retention can aim to be *licensed or acquired*, not only to build everything itself. Animaj in Paris is both the closest competitor model ("AI pipeline + platform-native IP") and a potential partner or acquirer.
- **Second-IP risk**: CoComelon's Netflix viewing fell about 60% while its narrative spinoff (*CoComelon Lane*) outperformed. Pinkfong deliberately built Bebefinn to cut dependence on Baby Shark (50% → 25% of revenue). A franchise needs a "family" of formats around the same characters (songs → stories → spinoffs).

## 2. From channel to franchise: when merch started, who made it, first products, platforms, live shows

### Takeaway
Merch tends to start through **one master toy licensee** (Jazwares, Spin Master, WowWee, Creative Kids, Kidz Toyz). Plush, musical or "talking" dolls and bedtime items are the usual first products, and they arrive when the channel has 1–2M subscribers (direct-to-fan: Lucas, Hey Bear) or tens of millions (mass retail: CoComelon, Ms Rachel). Streaming licences (Netflix, then Disney+) and live tours follow the toys. Feature films come last, 5–10 years in.

## 3. Episode format, structure and localization

### Takeaway
Two formats dominate:
- **Short songs (about 1.5–3 min) bundled into long compilations (30–60+ min)** for nursery-rhyme brands (CoComelon, LBB, Pinkfong, Super Simple, Gracie's, Ms Rachel on Netflix).
- **Short, wordless or near-wordless slapstick or story episodes (1–7 min)** for character comedies that travel globally (Om Nom, Masha, Pocoyo, Lucas, Xilam's Oggy/Zig & Sharko).

Localization uses either separate language channels (Masha 13+, Pocoyo 18, Pinkfong 25 languages, LBB 13) or, increasingly, YouTube multi-language audio tracks on a single video.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **For Lulu, a 1m50 song is the right "atom"**, but the money comes from compilations (YouTube long-form watch time, and streamers license compilations). Plan 20–60 minute bedtime compilations early.

Songs need dubbing, but the multi-audio feature now lets one video carry FR/EN/ES/PT/AR tracks, so audience accumulates on one asset instead of being split across channels.
- **Human or high-quality vocals matter for songs.** The evidence that AI dubs underperform comes from a vendor that sells human dubbing, so it is biased. Still, it suggests testing AI dubs against human dubs on 3–5 videos before scaling.
- **Setting refresh** (Om Nom changing setting every few seasons, Pocoyo adding sister Bea) keeps a long-running catalog fresh while keeping the core character.

## 4. Attachment mechanisms: why toddlers and preschoolers bond with a character

### Takeaway
Research and industry practice point to the same few factors:
- a face with "baby schema" proportions (round head, big low-set eyes)
- one simple, highly recognizable silhouette and colour code
- heavy repetition (same songs and episodes, same routines)
- direct address and pauses that invite participation
- a character with human-like needs (gets sleepy or hungry), which supports a one-way "friend" relationship
- a physical comfort object (plush) that carries the bond off-screen

Bedtime and comfort roles fit the age window when attachment to comfort objects peaks (18–24 months).

### Cited Findings
- Sandra Calvert (Georgetown) found that children treat characters such as Dora or Elmo as personal friends and learn and socialize through them. Parasocial bonding was once thought to be unique to adults. — [Georgetown, 2012 (older data)](https://college.georgetown.edu/news-story/technology-for-tots/)
- Calvert et al., *Child Development* (2019), 217 children aged 3–6: children with **stronger emotional feelings for the character**, and who talked more with it, gave faster and more accurate math answers. Learning transferred better when the game had an **embodied character** rather than a plain voiceover, and when the character gave **socially contingent replies**. — [SRCD press release, 2019-11-20](https://www.srcd.org/print/pdf/node/1017)
- Factors that shape a young child's parasocial relationship with a non-human character: (1) attachment, (2) character personification, (3) social realism, plus (4) **"humanlike needs", meaning the child's belief that the favorite character can get hungry or sleepy**. The source page timed out, so this is a snippet only. — [Georgetown CDMC (snippet)](https://cdmc.georgetown.edu/?p=59)
- *Blue's Clues* aired the **same episode five days in a row** on advice from psychologist Dan Anderson. Children silent on the first viewing were actively participating by the end of the week. The format used pauses for answers and direct address. — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/why-blues-clues-repeated-same-episode-five-days)
- The underlying study is Anderson, Bryant et al. (2000), "Researching Blue's Clues: Viewing behavior and impact", *Media Psychology* 2(2):179–194. — [search summary](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/attachments/other-applications-petitions-and-requests/attachment7.pdf)
- **Kindchenschema** (Lorenz, 1943): a large round head, high forehead, big low-set eyes, chubby cheeks and soft rounded proportions trigger "cute" responses and caretaking motivation. In an experiment with 122 participants, high baby-schema faces were rated cuter and drew stronger caretaking motivation. Characters like Mickey or BB-8 sit on this spectrum. — [Glocker et al. / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3596402); [UPenn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/cog_neuro_pubs/11)
- Lucas the Spider's premise was proving that "spiders could in fact be cute" (big eyes and a child's voice applied to an unlikely animal). — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2018/06/07/lucas-the-spider-plushie-sales/)
- **Transitional objects** (Winnicott): a soft toy or blanket helps a child self-soothe and separate at bedtime. Attachment to comfort objects usually begins around 6 months and peaks at 18–24 months. Moderate-quality health-news source. — [Advocate Health news (snippet)](https://www.ahchealthenews.com/?p=54126)
- Ms Rachel: a fixed visual identity (pink headband, overalls, high ponytail) and a "slowed-down and enthusiastic tone" based on speech-development research. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/29/ms-rachel-multimillionaire-toddler-sensation-personal-brand-secrets)
- Baby Shark: repetition plus easy hand choreography children can imitate, amplified by K-pop performers. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Shark)
- CoComelon/Moonbug attention testing (2022 NYT, reported second-hand): toddlers watch prototypes next to a "Distractatron" screen showing mundane scenes. When eyes stray, editors tweak the content, for example with faster cuts. Moonbug researchers even track preferences such as red vs yellow school buses. In 2026 CoComelon reportedly turned to UCLA to show its videos are not addictive (page unavailable). — [Conflicted Substack (secondary)](https://conflicted.substack.com/p/cocomelons-history-and-controversy); [secondary page, 2026 (unavailable)](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/06/cocomelon-turns-to-ucla-to-prove-its-videos-arent-addictive/)
- Backlash and regulation: the 2026 Fairplay letter describes AI kids' content as causing "attention hijacking" and notes that young children cannot read AI labels. A California jury found YouTube liable for engagement-maximizing design without regard to youth wellbeing. — [WTOP/AP](https://wtop.com/social-media/2026/04/advocacy-groups-urge-youtube-to-protect-kids-from-ai-slop-videos/)

### Inferences
- **Lulu's design already ticks most boxes:** round body (baby schema), one signature colour cue (pink bow), a unique feature children can name and imitate (the glowing belly, the pompom antennae), and a natural "humanlike need" (sleepiness, which is the bedtime theme itself). Nino gives a child to identify with, much as Masha or JJ do.
- **Mechanisms to make explicit in scripts:**
  - a fixed opening ritual (the same 5–10 sec "Lulu lights up" sting)
  - a recurring catchphrase and gesture children can copy (e.g., "on éteint, on s'endort" plus a hand gesture)
  - direct address with real pauses (Blue's Clues, Ms Rachel)
  - the same song reused across many videos (Blue's Clues repetition)
  - Lulu showing needs (yawning, getting sleepy) so children "care for" her
- **Calming, not hooking, is a strategic differentiator.** CoComelon's fast-cut, attention-maximizing approach is under criticism and regulatory pressure. A bedtime IP that is *explicitly calm* (slow tempo, few cuts, a dimming palette) puts itself on the right side of that trend and of French health guidance (section 6).
- **A plush is the "bridge" object**: comfort objects peak at 18–24 months, and a plush is the item parents can justify (sleep aid rather than screen time). That supports a glowing-belly plush or nightlight as the hero product.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that isolates "looking at the camera" in animated (non-host) characters for 2–4 year-olds. Evidence comes from host-led shows (Blue's Clues, Ms Rachel).
- The full Georgetown paper on "humanlike needs" could not be read (timeout); the factor list comes from a search snippet.
- The 2026 UCLA/CoComelon study details were not retrieved.

## 5. Under-exploited angles in 2026: niches without a dominant IP, and what a small automated studio could own

### Takeaway
English-language nursery rhymes and general preschool learning are saturated by giants (CoComelon 200M subscribers, Pinkfong 300M across channels, LBB 195M, Super Simple 47M). Openings remain in four areas:
- **calm bedtime/sleep content with a recurring character** (no dominant IP found; Moshi and lullaby channels are mostly characterless or audio-only)
- **audio-first formats** (tonies at €630M revenue; Lunii in France), where screen-free content is what parents want
- **babies 0–2** (Hey Bear Sensory proves a niche IP can license at about 2M subscribers)
- **non-English originals dubbed outward** (Pinkfong's Korean content at +296% with 90% overseas viewers)

The main 2026 constraint is platform policy against "mass-produced" AI kids' content, so automation must stay invisible behind authored, consistent IP.

### Inferences
- **The most defensible space for Orbeo is "calm bedtime character IP, audio-first, screen-light".** No YouTube-born character dominates bedtime. Big brands treat bedtime as a sub-theme (a doll, a playset, "lullaby compilations"), and the sleep apps (Moshi, Calm) have weak characters for toddlers. Lulu (a light that dims at night) is a natural owner of the "rituel du coucher" with a built-in product hook: a glowing plush or nightlight, audio stories, and lullaby albums.
- **Audio products pay better and match the bedtime use.** A Lulu "audio bedtime ritual" (5–15 min stories plus lullabies) can be distributed through Spotify/Deezer/Apple, podcasts, and later through tonies/Lunii/Yoto-style licensing. Automated audio production (TTS plus music) is also more mature and cheaper than video.
- **Age-band focus:** 0–2 (sensory and lullabies, Hey Bear-like) and 2–4 (bedtime routine songs with Nino) are less crowded than 3–6 learning songs. Pick one primary band; Lulu and Nino suggest 2–4 with lullabies that also work for 0–2.
- **Automation must be invisible.** To avoid being classed as "inauthentic, mass-produced" (demonetization) or blocked from YouTube Kids, the channel needs:
  - a consistent authored character bible
  - recurring storylines and original songs, not template variants
  - visible human curation (credits, a creator voice, a parent-facing brand)
  - a modest cadence of high-quality episodes rather than daily volume
  
  Animaj shows investors reward "AI pipeline + strong IP", not AI volume.
- **Licensing on an IP built with AI may face extra scrutiny**, from toy licensees and from copyright eligibility of AI-generated elements. Orbeo should keep human-authored character sheets, song lyrics and melodies as the documented IP core (to cross-check with the IP research thread).

## 6. What applies to a French-language start

### Takeaway
French-language kids' YouTube is big enough to build a top channel: Titounis at 7.44M subscribers, Kidi Fun at 6.28M with only 388 videos, Masha et Michka at 12M, Xilam's wordless shows at 43M+ combined. But the strongest French successes either travel without words (Xilam) or are bilingual (Kidi Fun). France also has two strategic specifics:
- **official guidance against screens before age 3 and before sleep**, which pushes a bedtime IP towards audio
- **a home-grown audio-device ecosystem** (Lunii) and a French AI-kids studio (Animaj) as possible partners or benchmarks

### Cited Findings
- France's top YouTube channels (Aug 2026) include Zig & Sharko (16.6M, #3), Oggy (13.9M, #7) and Grizzy & les Lemmings (12.7M, #8), all wordless Xilam animation with 43M+ combined subscribers, plus Masha et Michka (12M, #10). — [Digitiz, 2026-08-05](https://digitiz.fr/chaines-youtube-france/)

### Inferences
- **The positioning conflict to resolve:** a YouTube *bedtime* channel for 2–4 year-olds runs directly against French guidance ("never screens before sleep"; no screens under 3). French parents, pediatricians, crèches and possibly French press and retail could treat "Lulu bedtime videos" as a bad practice. **Recommended framing:**
  - YouTube and Shorts serve as the *discovery and character-building* window (daytime songs, the "getting ready for bed" routine, brushing teeth, pajamas)
  - the actual *sleep moment* is audio-only: lullabies and stories on Spotify/Deezer/Apple Music/podcasts, later Lunii/tonies/Yoto
  - plus the glowing plush or nightlight
  
  This turns a liability into a differentiator ("the character who turns off the screen").
- **French-language originals with an international plan:** the French market alone supports channels at 6–7M subscribers (Titounis, Kidi Fun), probably helped by Francophone audiences beyond France, though this was not verified. Orbeo can make French the "original version" (a selling point for French institutions such as Bpifrance, the CNC and France TV's Okoo) and add EN/ES/PT tracks with multi-audio. Using few words, with songs carried by melody and gesture, keeps dubbing cheap.
- **Wordless gags travel best from France** (Xilam's three channels at 43M+). For story episodes (not songs), Lulu could carry mostly non-verbal light, sound and gesture gags, with Nino using simple words.
- **Local partner and exit options:** Lunii (audio content licensing), Animaj (production or acquisition of platform-born IP), French publishers already used to licensing (Hachette and Larousse publish Masha), and tonies (licenses YouTube-born brands such as Masha).
- **Low video count can still win:** Kidi Fun reaches 6.28M subscribers with 388 videos, which suggests that original, memorable songs beat volume. That supports a slower, quality-first cadence, which is also safer under YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule.
