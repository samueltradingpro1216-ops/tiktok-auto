## 1. What exact prompt anatomy does each vendor recommend (order of elements)?

### Takeaway
Every vendor converges on the same backbone — style/shot first or early, then subject (with a fixed, distinctive description), action as timed beats, setting, camera, lighting/palette, and audio described separately at the end or in its own block — but the containers differ: Veo/Seedance/Wan give a one-line "formula", Sora and xAI use labeled plain-text blocks (Cinematography / Actions / Dialogue / Sound / Continuity), Kling and Wan use bracketed speaker tags, and LTX explicitly wants a single flowing present-tense paragraph.

### Cited Findings

**Google Veo 3.1 (Vertex AI / Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform blog)**
- Five-part formula: "[Cinematography] + [Subject] + [Action] + [Context] + [Style & Ambiance]" with the example: "Medium shot, a tired corporate worker, rubbing his temples in exhaustion, in front of a bulky 1980s computer in a cluttered office late at night. The scene is lit by the harsh fluorescent overhead lights and the green glow of the monochrome monitor. Retro aesthetic, shot as if on 1980s color film, slightly grainy." — [Google Cloud blog, "The ultimate prompting guide for Veo 3.1" (Oct 16, 2025)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- The blog calls the [Cinematography] element "the most powerful tool for conveying tone and emotion" — [same](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- The Vertex prompt guide (now titled for "Gemini Omni Flash and Veo") lists elements: Subject, Action, Scene or context, Camera angles, Camera movements, Lens and optical effects, Visual style & aesthetics (Lighting, Tone or mood, Artistic style, Ambiance), Temporal elements, Audio, Cinematic terms, Negative prompts. "You don't need to use all elements in every prompt." — [Vertex AI video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide) (last updated 2026-10-15)
- Gemini API "Prompt writing basics": Subject, Action, Style, then [Optional] Camera positioning and motion, Composition, Focus and lens effects, Ambiance; tips "Use descriptive language" and "Enhance the facial details… like using the word portrait" — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)

**Gemini Omni Flash (gemini-omni-1.1-flash)**
- "Write prompts with details like scene description, camera movement, lighting and mood for best results." By default "Omni Flash will try to create a video with a few different shots… If you need the output video to contain a single scene, you must prompt for that: In a single unbroken scene / In a single continuous shot / No scene cuts". Official example: "Continuous, unbroken handheld shot of a fluffy tabby cat sitting on a sunny windowsill, looking out into a leafy garden. The cat's tail twitches slowly, and its ears rotate slightly toward ambient noises. Sunbeams illuminate dust motes in the air. Sound design: Gentle breeze, distant bird chirps. No dialogue." — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- "Meta prompting" is officially supported, e.g. "Consider micro-detail, expression and timing to create a very rich, detailed but entirely natural scene." / "Be extremely detailed in your descriptions of characters and environments. Apply costume design principles to characters." — [same](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)

**OpenAI Sora 2 (archived guide, model now shut down in API)**
- "A clear prompt describes a shot as if you were sketching it onto a storyboard. State the camera framing, note depth of field, describe the action in beats, and set the lighting and palette. Anchoring your subject with a few distinctive details keeps it recognizable, while a single, plausible action makes the shot easier to follow." — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide (cookbook, updated March 2026, archived)](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Official "Descriptive Prompt Template" (verbatim):
```
[Prose scene description in plain language. Describe characters, costumes, scenery, weather and other details. Be as descriptive to generate a video that matches your vision.]

Cinematography:
Camera shot: [framing and angle, e.g. wide establishing shot, eye level]
Mood: [overall tone, e.g. cinematic and tense, playful and suspenseful, luxurious anticipation]

Actions:
- [Action 1: a clear, specific beat or gesture]
- [Action 2: another distinct beat within the clip]
- [Action 3: another action or dialogue line]

Dialogue:
[If the shot has dialogue, add short natural lines here or as part of the actions list. Keep them brief so they match the clip length.]
```
— [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- "Establish this style early so the model can carry it through consistently." Weak/strong table: "Cinematic look" → "Anamorphic 2.0x lens, shallow DOF, volumetric light"; "A beautiful street at night" → "Wet asphalt, zebra crosswalk, neon signs reflecting in puddles" — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Official Example 1 (verbatim): "Style: Hand-painted 2D/3D hybrid animation with soft brush textures, warm tungsten lighting, and a tactile, stop-motion feel. The aesthetic evokes mid-2000s storybook animation — cozy, imperfect, full of mechanical charm. Subtle watercolor wash and painterly textures; warm–cool balance in grade; filmic motion blur for animated realism. / Inside a cluttered workshop, shelves overflow with gears, bolts, and yellowing blueprints. At the center, a small round robot sits on a wooden bench, its dented body patched with mismatched plates and old paint layers. Its large glowing eyes flicker pale blue as it fiddles nervously with a humming light bulb. The air hums with quiet mechanical whirs, rain patters on the window, and the clock ticks steadily in the background. / Cinematography: Camera: medium close-up, slow push-in with gentle parallax from hanging tools / Lens: 35 mm virtual lens; shallow depth of field to soften background clutter / Lighting: warm key from overhead practical; cool spill from window for contrast / Mood: gentle, whimsical, a touch of suspense / Actions: - The robot taps the bulb; sparks crackle. - It flinches, dropping the bulb, eyes widening. - The bulb tumbles in slow motion; it catches it just in time. - A puff of steam escapes its chest — relief and pride. - Robot says quietly: "Almost lost it… but I got it!" / Background Sound: Rain, ticking clock, soft mechanical hum, faint bulb sizzle." (line breaks shown as " / ") — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Official Example 2 (verbatim, abridged line breaks): "Style: 1970s romantic drama, shot on 35 mm film with natural flares, soft focus, and warm halation. Slight gate weave and handheld micro-shake evoke vintage intimacy. Warm Kodak-inspired grade; light halation on bulbs; film grain and soft vignette for period authenticity. / At golden hour, a brick tenement rooftop transforms into a small stage. … A young woman in a flowing red silk dress dances barefoot, curls glowing in the fading light. Her partner — sleeves rolled, suspenders loose — claps along, his smile wide and unguarded. Below, the city hums with car horns, subway tremors, and distant laughter. / Cinematography: Camera: medium-wide shot, slow dolly-in from eye level / Lens: 40 mm spherical; shallow focus to isolate the couple from skyline / Lighting: golden natural key with tungsten bounce; edge from fairy bulbs / Mood: nostalgic, tender, cinematic / Actions: - She spins; her dress flares, catching sunlight. - Woman (laughing): "See? Even the city dances with us tonight." - He steps in, catches her hand, and dips her into shadow. - Man (smiling): "Only because you lead." - Sheets drift across frame, briefly veiling the skyline before parting again. / Background Sound: Natural ambience only: faint wind, fabric flutter, street noise, muffled music. No added score." — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- API guide short form: "For best results, describe shot type, subject, action, setting, and lighting", e.g. "Wide shot of a child flying a red kite in a grassy park, golden hour sunlight, camera slowly pans upward." — [OpenAI Video generation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/video-generation.md)

**xAI Grok Imagine Video 1.5**
- Text-to-video: "Describe the shot, including camera movement, lighting, and sound. On grok-imagine-video-1.5 and grok-imagine-video-1.5-lite, the model renders a first frame from the prompt, then animates it." — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)
- xAI's own showcase prompts are long, labeled plain-text "maxi prompts" with blocks "Shot 1…", "Hard cut…", "Sound:", "Continuity:", "Lighting:", "Skin:". Verbatim excerpt of the official Lena/Sam reference-to-video dialogue prompt: "The woman from <IMAGE_0> is Lena. The man from <IMAGE_1> is Sam. They sit across the round marble table by the rainy street window in the café from <IMAGE_2>; between them are two cups of coffee and one small plate holding exactly two croissants. Two shots joined by a single hard cut, no cross-dissolve, no fade. Sam stays on the left of the frame and Lena on the right in both shots. Both blink and breathe naturally, with natural pauses between lines. / Shot 1, wide two-shot from the café side, rainy window behind them. They look at each other with easy, soft smiles. Lena holds her cup in both hands, calm and a little reserved. Sam is buzzing with energy; he leans in, eyebrows lifting, grinning, one hand gesturing as he says, [line] … Hard cut, no cross-dissolve, to an over-the-shoulder shot from behind Sam … Lena takes a moment before reacting. Her smile lingers, then slowly fades. She glances down at her cup, then back up at him, brow drawing in slightly. She sets the cup down carefully and swallows. Quietly, cautious, voice tight: [line] … Sound: a busy café under the dialogue throughout, with espresso machine hiss, clinking cups, chatter and rain on the window, never silent between lines. / Continuity: café, table, two cups, the plate with exactly two croissants, and the window match in both shots; nobody touches the croissants; nothing appears, disappears or changes shape. Natural lip sync for each speaker. / Lighting: natural uneven room lighting, cozy cafe, faces unevenly lit. / Skin: natural pores and skin texture. Uneven indoor lighting, not studio style." The dialogue shown with that example: "Sam: "I took the job in Berlin. We could leave in March." Lena: "We? You didn't even ask me." Sam: "I thought you'd be happy for me." Lena: "I am. (15 s, 720p) — [xAI Video Overview, "Show prompt" content extracted from page source](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)

**Alibaba Wan (official Model Studio guide)**
- Basic: "Prompt = Entity + Scene + Motion". Advanced: "Prompt = Entity (description) + Scene (description) + Motion (description) + Aesthetic control + Stylization", and "Most weak prompts cover entity and motion but skip aesthetic control and stylization — producing a static camera in an undefined void. Cover all five dimensions." Aesthetic control "Includes light source, lighting environment, shot size, camera angle, lens, and camera movement" and "Each camera move carries an emotional intent: a push-in creates intimacy or tension, a pull-out reveals scale or isolation…" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, Video generation prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)

- Official Wan examples often begin with a comma-separated style/camera header, e.g. "Moonlight, soft lighting, side lighting, medium close-up shot, center composition. Inside a cozy children's bedroom…" — [same](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)

**Lightricks LTX-2 / 2.3 / 2.5**
- Official repo: "focus on detailed, chronological descriptions of actions and scenes. Include specific movements, appearances, camera angles, and environmental details - all in a single flowing paragraph. Start directly with the action, and keep descriptions literal and precise. Think like a cinematographer describing a shot list. Keep within 200 words." Structure: "Start with main action in a single sentence / Add specific details about movements and gestures / Describe character/object appearances precisely / Include background and environment details / Specify camera angles and movements / Describe lighting and colors / Note any changes or sudden events" — [LTX-2 GitHub README](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2)
- LTX blog guide: Key aspects "Establish the shot… Set the scene… Describe the action… Define your character(s)… Identify camera movement(s)… Describe the audio." For best results: "Keep your prompt in a single flowing paragraph… Use present tense verbs… Match your detail to the shot scale. Closeups need more precise detail than wide shots… You should expect to write 4 to 8 descriptive sentences" — [LTX blog prompting guide for LTX-2 (Dec 11, 2025)](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2)
- "Stylized aesthetics: Painterly, noir, analog film look… work especially well when named early in the prompt." — [same](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2)
- Third-party summary of the LTX docs: six elements in one flowing present-tense paragraph — shot, scene, action, character, camera movement, audio — [Dream Pixel Forge](https://www.dreampixelforge.com/blog/ltx-prompts) (third party; the official docs.ltx.video page returned 403 to my fetch)

**MiniMax H3**
- Official docs describe modes (T2V, first/last frame I2V, reference generation for "character, motion, camera, style, voice, or editing rhythm") but I found no official prompt anatomy; the only official example prompt is "The character performs street dance, following the motion in reference video 1; the character's appearance follows reference images 1 and 2." — [MiniMax video generation guide](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/video-generation)

### Inferences
- A cross-vendor "maxi prompt" skeleton consistent with all official docs: (1) style/format line; (2) fixed character block(s) with distinctive anchors; (3) setting + light + palette; (4) camera (shot size, angle, lens, one move); (5) actions as numbered/timed beats with dialogue embedded at the beat where it is spoken; (6) audio block (ambience, SFX, music or "no music"); (7) continuity/constraints ("no subtitles", "only these two characters speak"). For LTX, collapse the same content into one present-tense paragraph with audio last.
- Labeled blocks (Sora, xAI) and one-paragraph prose (LTX) are both official; the label style is a plain-text convention, not JSON.

### Gaps
- docs.ltx.video's own prompting guide page returned HTTP 403 (Cloudflare), so I relied on the LTX GitHub README and ltx.io blog.
- No official MiniMax H3 / Hailuo prompt anatomy found; no official Seedance 2.0 prompt guide on BytePlus (only 1.0 and 1.5 guides are listed).
- Google Flow help-center pages were not fetched.

## 2. How should dialogue be written (speaker attribution, quotes, language/French, accents, voice) so the right character lip-syncs, without subtitles or extra speakers? How long can a line be for 8–10 s?

### Takeaway
All vendors want the spoken words verbatim in quotes, attached to a uniquely named/described speaker at the moment they act, with voice/tone/pace/accent described next to the line; Kling and Alibaba officially publish the same four rules (unique labels, action-before-line anchoring, distinct voice labels, linking words like "Immediately,"). French is the weak point: Kling 3.0 officially translates unsupported languages (French included) into English, Seedance 1.5 Pro's list omits French, Veo/Omni only "fully support" English, while LTX, Wan and Kling allow naming the language/accent explicitly. Sora's official timing rule — 4 s ≈ 1–2 short exchanges, 8 s "a few more" — is the only explicit line-length guidance.

### Cited Findings

**Veo 3 / 3.1**
- Vertex guide colon syntax: "Dialogue: spoken words from characters or a narrator. For example, "the man in the red hat says: Where is the rabbit?", "a voiceover with a polished British accent speaks in a serious, urgent tone", "two people discuss a movie."" Official example: "A medium shot in a dimly lit interrogation room. The seasoned detective says: Your story has holes. The nervous informant, sweating under a single bare bulb, replies: I'm telling you everything I know. The only other sounds are the slow, rhythmic ticking of a wall clock and the faint sound of rain against the window" — [Vertex AI video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide)
- Veo 3.1 blog and Gemini API instead use quotation marks: "Dialogue: Use quotation marks for specific speech (e.g., A woman says, "We have to leave now.")." — [Veo 3.1 prompting blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1); "Dialogue: Use quotes for specific speech. (Example: "This must be the key," he murmured.)" — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- Official Gemini API dialogue example with screenplay-style labels and parenthetical acting: "A wide shot of a misty Pacific Northwest forest. Two exhausted hikers, a man and a woman, push through ferns when the man stops abruptly, staring at a tree. Close-up: Fresh, deep claw marks are gouged into the tree's bark. Man: (Hand on his hunting knife) "That's no ordinary bear." Woman: (Voice tight with fear, scanning the woods) "Then what is it?" A rough bark, snapping twigs, footsteps on the damp earth. A lone bird chirps." and the minimal one: "Paper Cut-Out Animation. New Librarian: "Where do you keep the forbidden books?" Old Curator: "We don't. They keep us."" — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- Official code-sample prompt: "A close up of two people staring at a cryptic drawing on a wall, torchlight flickering. A man murmurs, 'This must be it. That's the secret code.' The woman looks at him and whispering excitedly, 'What did you find?'" — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- Language: "English (EN) is fully supported, but other languages have not been evaluated, so they may work but results can vary." (Veo 3.1 limitations; same sentence on Omni Flash) — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo); [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- Extension caveat: "Note that voice is not able to be effectively extended if it's not present in the last 1 second of video." — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- Subtitles: MIT Technology Review reported Veo 3 "often adds nonsensical, garbled subtitles" to dialogue clips "even when the prompts it's been given explicitly ask for no captions or subtitles to be added", attributing it partly to negative prompts being less effective than positive ones — [MIT Technology Review, July 15, 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/15/1120156/googles-generative-video-model-veo-3-has-a-subtitles-problem/). Kapwing suggests ending the prompt with "Do not include subtitles or on-screen text" and says it is "not foolproof" — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-add-accurate-subtitles-to-veo-3-google-gemini-videos/) (third party). Claims that words like "dialogue"/"speaking" trigger captions come from SEO blogs and are unverified — [vidau.ai](https://www.vidau.ai/veo-3-prompt-no-subtitles-for-beautiful-video-outcome/) (third party).

**Gemini Omni Flash**
- "Removing unwanted elements… include simple negative prompts to avoid them: No dialogue / No embellishments / No extra sound effects"; negative-prompt parameter "not supported (you can put your negatives in the regular prompt: e.g., "Do not do X")" — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- "Uploading audio references is unsupported in the current version of the API"; "You cannot extend an uploaded video where someone is talking to add additional dialogue… multi-turn extension with previous_interaction_id can be used" — [same](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)

**Sora 2 (archived)**
- "Dialogue must be described directly in your prompt. Place it in a <dialogue> block below your prose description so the model clearly distinguishes visual description from spoken lines. Keep lines concise and natural, and try to limit exchanges to a handful of sentences so the timing can match your clip length. For multi-character scenes, label speakers consistently and use alternating turns; this helps the model associate each line with the correct character's gestures and expressions." — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- "a 4-second shot will usually accommodate one or two short exchanges, while an 8-second clip can support a few more. Long, complex speeches are unlikely to sync well and may break pacing." — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)

A single bare bulb dangles from the ceiling, its light pooling onto the scarred metal table at the center. Two chairs face each other across it. On one side sits the Detective, trench coat draped across the back of his chair, eyes sharp and unblinking. Across from him, the Suspect slouches, cigarette smoke curling lazily toward the ceiling. The silence presses in, broken only by the faint hum of the overhead light. / Dialogue: - Detective: "You're lying. I can hear it in your silence." - Suspect: "Or maybe I'm just tired of talking." - Detective: "Either way, you'll talk before the night's over."" — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)

### Inferences
- Best cross-model pattern for a French TikTok clip: name each speaker with a stable descriptive label (e.g. "[Mamie, voix douce et chevrotante]"), put the visible action first, then the quoted French line, declare "speaks in French, with a Parisian accent" (or similar) next to the line, link turns with "Immediately," / "Then", end the audio block with "Only [names] speak; no narrator, no other voices" and an explicit "No subtitles, no on-screen text" (in a negative field where one exists).
- For French specifically: avoid Kling 3.0 for French speech (official doc says it translates to English), treat Seedance 1.5 as unsupported for French, and treat Veo 3.1 / Omni Flash / Wan / LTX / Grok as "may work, test it" because no vendor officially lists French as evaluated except via LTX metadata tags.
- Line length (inference, not an official number): Sora's "4 s ≈ 1–2 short exchanges" implies roughly 8–12 spoken words per 4 s; for an 8 s clip aim for ~15–20 French words total, and for 10 s ~20–25, leaving room for a beat of silence at the start and end. LTX-2.3's "timing collapses" warning supports splitting longer lines into phrases with pauses or across clips.
- Because Veo extension cannot carry a voice not present in the last second, end clips that will be extended mid-speech or on a held line.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes an official words-per-second or max-characters-per-line rule (only Sora's qualitative 4 s / 8 s rule).
- No official or credible test of French lip-sync quality was found for any model (Veo, Omni, Grok, Wan, LTX, Seedance 2.0, MiniMax H3).
- No official Google guidance on suppressing subtitles beyond generic negative-prompt advice.

## 3. What do guides say about prompt length? Is there a point where longer prompts hurt?

### Takeaway
Hard limits differ by more than an order of magnitude (Veo 3.1: 1,024 text tokens; MiniMax H3: 7,000 characters; LTX-2 README: "within 200 words"), and official advice splits: Sora warns long prompts restrict the model and may not be followed reliably, LTX-2.3 says longer descriptive prompts "consistently outperform" short ones, Omni Flash and xAI showcase very long prompts, while LTX-2, Wan and Omni (for editing) warn that piling on actions/characters/instructions drops elements.

### Cited Findings
- Veo 3.1, 3.1 Fast and 3.1 Lite: "Limits — Text input 1,024 tokens" — [Gemini API Veo page, Model versions](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- MiniMax H3: "Prompt length limit ≤ 7000 characters" — [MiniMax video generation guide](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/video-generation)
- LTX-2: "Keep within 200 words" — [LTX-2 GitHub README](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2); "You should expect to write 4 to 8 descriptive sentences" — [LTX blog prompting guide](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2)
- LTX-2.3: "prompt length matters more: longer, more descriptive prompts consistently outperform short ones on LTX-2.3, because the model has more anchors to align the generation against", but "Over-directing every micro-beat… If every phrase carries three cues, the model has nothing to prioritize and the performance reads as twitchy. One direction per beat is the working limit." — [LTX blog, Directing Dialogue and Acting](https://ltx.io/blog/directing-dialogue-and-acting) (contradicts the older "within 200 words" README line; the README section is titled "Prompting for LTX-2")
- LTX-2: "Over complicated prompts: The more actions/ characters/ instructions you add, the higher the chance some of them won't be seen in the output. Begin with simple things and layer on additional instructions as you iterate." and "Scene complexity overload: Too many characters, layered actions, or excessive objects reduce clarity and model accuracy." — [LTX blog prompting guide](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2)
- Sora 2: "Shorter prompts give the model more creative freedom. Expect surprising results. Longer, more detailed prompts restrict the model's creativity. It will try to follow your guidance, but might not always do so reliably." Also "Treat your prompt as a creative wish list, not a contract." and "If a shot keeps misfiring, strip it back: freeze the camera, simplify the action, clear the background." — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Sora 2's own "Going Ultra-Detailed" production-brief example (Format & Look / Lenses & Filtration / Grade / Lighting & Atmosphere / Location & Framing / Wardrobe / Sound / Optimized Shot List / Camera Notes / Finishing) is ~400 words for a 4 s clip, e.g. "Sound — Diegetic only: faint rail screech, train brakes hiss, distant announcement muffled (-20 LUFS), low ambient hum. Footsteps and paper rustle; no score or added foley." — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Sora 2 duration: "The model generally follows instructions more reliably in shorter clips… you may see better results by stitching together two 4 second clips in editing instead of generating a single 8 second clip." — [same](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Gemini Omni Flash: meta-prompts asking to "Be extremely detailed in your descriptions" are officially suggested for generation, but for editing "Simple prompts work best… Overly descriptive prompts can lead to unintended changes." — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- Veo (Gemini API) shows "Less detail" vs "More detail" pairs and frames more detail as refinement, e.g. the detailed "A close-up cinematic shot follows a desperate man in a weathered green trench coat as he dials a rotary phone…" — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- Wan: "Very long or complex action sequences — A character performing a 30-second choreographed sequence. Keep actions simple and short"; optional `prompt_extend` "expands short prompts… into detailed, structured descriptions" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)
- Grok Imagine: no prompt length limit published in the pages I read; official showcase prompts run to several hundred words (the 14-reference loft prompt is ~500 words) — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); [xAI video generation guide](https://docs.x.ai/docs/guides/video-generations)
- Third party on MiniMax H3: "clearer control tends to beat longer prompts" — [Leadde](https://leadde.ai/blog/how-to-use-mini-max-h3) (third party)

### Inferences
- For Veo 3.1 a 1,024-token cap is roughly 700–800 English words; a French maxi prompt should stay well below that (French tokenizes less efficiently than English — general LLM tokenizer behaviour, not verified for Veo).
- "Long" helps when it adds anchors (fixed character traits, continuity constraints, explicit audio), and hurts when it adds more simultaneous actions, more characters, or conflicting cues. One camera move and one main action per shot is the shared ceiling.

### Gaps
- No published prompt limits found for Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, Wan 2.5+, Grok Imagine, LTX-2.3/2.5 (beyond the README's 200 words) or Gemini Omni Flash.
- No controlled study on prompt length vs adherence for any of these models.

## 4. Official tips for multi-shot sequences and timestamps within one prompt

### Takeaway
Timestamped or numbered shot blocks are officially supported by Veo 3.1 ("timestamp prompting"), Gemini Omni Flash (natural-language timing or [0-3s] timecodes; it cuts by default), Wan 2.6+ ("Shot 1 [0–3 s]"), Kling 3.0 (Multi-Shot / Custom Multi-Shot up to 15 s), Kling 3.0 Omni ("Shot 1 (3s):"), Seedance 1.5 ("Shot 1: … Shot 2: Cut to…") and LTX-2.5; Sora recommends distinct shot blocks but also suggests stitching short clips.

### Cited Findings
- Veo 3.1 timestamp prompting (verbatim official example): "[00:00-00:02] Medium shot from behind a young female explorer with a leather satchel and messy brown hair in a ponytail, as she pushes aside a large jungle vine to reveal a hidden path. [00:02-00:04] Reverse shot of the explorer's freckled face, her expression filled with awe as she gazes upon ancient, moss-covered ruins in the background. SFX: The rustle of dense leaves, distant exotic bird calls. [00:04-00:06] Tracking shot following the explorer as she steps into the clearing and runs her hand over the intricate carvings on a crumbling stone wall. Emotion: Wonder and reverence. [00:06-00:08] Wide, high-angle crane shot, revealing the lone explorer standing small in the center of the vast, forgotten temple complex, half-swallowed by the jungle. SFX: A swelling, gentle orchestral score begins to play." — [Veo 3.1 prompting blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Veo cinematic terms: "match cut", "jump cut", "establishing shot sequence", "montage", "split diopter effect" — [Vertex AI video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide)
- Omni Flash: "You can prompt for things to happen at specific times in the video, there is no precise syntax needed… After 3 seconds, a woman enters the scene. At 5s the chorus starts in the background audio. Every 2s cut to a new frame… You can also use a timecode syntax: [0-3s] A person is walking [3-6s] They stop and turn around [6-10s] They start running". Extensions: "extend videos by 10s, up to a total length of 40s"; when extending, "0s refers to the beginning of the extended part" — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- Sora 2: "Describing multiple shots in a single prompt is also valid… keep each shot block distinct: one camera setup, one subject action, and one lighting recipe at a time." Ultra-detailed example uses a timed shot list: "0.00–2.40 — "Arrival Drift" (32 mm, shoulder-mounted slow dolly left)… 2.40–4.00 — "Turn and Pause" (50 mm, slow arc in)" — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Kling 3.0: two modes, "Multi-Shot" (model "automatically plans the shot transitions") and "Custom Multi-Shot" ("precisely control the content and duration of each shot. The model will strictly follow the prompts"). Example: "shot 1, profile shot of black man driving a truck, cinematic handheld. Shot 2, frontal macro shot of black man driving a truck, cinematic handheld. Shot 3, macro shot of hands on the steering wheel, cinematic handheld. Shot 4, macro shot of a weathered picture of a young black child laying on the passenger side seat, cinematic handheld." Duration "flexible… ranging from 3 to 15 seconds". Single-take timing can also be given in prose: "At the 4th second, the camera accelerates forward with her… At the 8th second… At the 12th second… In the final 3 seconds…" and "This is a 15-second cinematic long take, a single unbroken shot with no edited transitions." — [Kling VIDEO 3.0 Model User Guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide)
- Kling 3.0 Omni example with per-shot durations and elements: "Shot 1 (3s): Mid-shot, background @Image. @Grace sits on the sofa eating cookies as @Alan walks in holding @Samoyed. @Samoyed lunges for the cookie in @Grace's hand. @Grace says, "Hey! Watch your dog!" Shot 2 (2s): @Alan sits beside her, pulling the leash and lifting @Samoyed. Close-up, @Alan says, "He just likes cookies more than me." Shot 3 (3s): Close-up, @Grace smiles and says, "Well, he has good taste at least."" — [Kling VIDEO 3.0 Omni Model User Guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-omni-model-user-guide)
- fal (partner) says Kling 3.0 storyboards support "up to six shots in a single output" — [fal Kling 3.0 guide](https://blog.fal.ai/kling-3-0-prompting-guide)
- Wan multi-shot (Wan 3.0/2.7/2.6): "Prompt = Overall description + Shot number + Timestamp + Shot content", example: "This story is a short play about giving up and regaining hope, told from a third-person perspective. Shot 1 [0–3 s] A boy sits alone in a corner of a playground, looking down at a letter in his hand. He sighs softly, his eyes filled with confusion. Shot 2 [4–6 s] Hard cut transition, fixed camera position, focusing on the boy's eyes, which are glistening with tears, showing loss and helplessness. Shot 3 [7–10 s] Hard cut transition, the scene changes to a simple classroom…". "Wan 2.7 no longer supports the shot_type parameter… To generate a single-shot video, write 'Generate single shot.'" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt) (the same page's avoid-table still says "Cuts happen between clips, not within them. One clip = one continuous shot", apparently written for older Wan versions)
- Seedance 1.5 Pro: "Supports reverse-shot editing for dialogue scenes" and "Supports responding to shot switching timing"; example: "Shot 1: The shot starts with a medium-long shot from the rooftop, where two people stand together in the frame above the city illuminated by neon lights. The male character says, "This city eats people alive." Shot 2: The shot cuts to a close-up of the female character, who sneers and replies, "Only if you let it." Shot 3: The shot cuts back to a close-up of the male character, who turns to look at the other, his tone turns solemn: "You really think we can change it?" Shot 4: The shot cuts to a medium-close shot of the female character, who steps forward, the neon light dancing on her face, and firmly says, "No. But we can survive it."…" — [BytePlus Seedance-1.5-pro prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-1-5-pro)
- LTX-2.5: native multi-shot in a single pass, auto duration; official repo lists a "Duration Head — optional; lets you omit --num-frames and have the clip length predicted from the prompt" — [LTX-2 GitHub README](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2); multi-shot launch details are third-party — [comfyui-wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-11-ltx-2-5-open-weights-release), [deAPI LTX-2.5 prompting guide](https://deapi.ai/blog/ltx-2-5-prompting-guide-multi-shot-scenes-native-audio-and-prompts-that-actually-cut) (third party)
- Grok Imagine: official dialogue example says "Two shots joined by a single hard cut, no cross-dissolve, no fade" and pins frames with "up to four keyframes" at chosen timestamps — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)

### Inferences
- A safe universal syntax is "Shot 1 [0–4 s]: … Shot 2 [4–8 s]: Hard cut to …", with each shot holding one camera setup, one action and at most one or two lines of dialogue; for Omni Flash and Kling Multi-Shot, explicitly say "single continuous shot, no cuts" when you do not want cuts, since they cut by default.
- For dialogue scenes, shot-reverse-shot is explicitly supported by Kling 3.0 and Seedance 1.5 and shown in Veo's ingredients workflow and xAI's café example.

### Gaps
- No official limit on shot count per clip except third-party "up to six" for Kling 3.0.
- No official multi-shot syntax published by Lightricks itself for LTX-2.5 (only third-party guides found).

## 5. How to keep a character identical across clips (character blocks, references, ingredients, cameos, elements)

### Takeaway
Text-only consistency relies on reusing an identical, distinctive character description word-for-word (Sora, Seedance, fal/Kling); every current model also has a reference mechanism — Veo 3.1 "ingredients" (≤3 images), Omni Flash image/video refs with tags, Kling 3.0 Elements with bound voice, Grok 1.5 (≤14 images + ≤3 voices), Seedance 2.0 / MiniMax H3 (≤9 images, ≤3 videos, ≤3 audio), Wan 2.6+ reference-to-video (voice timbre preserved), Sora Characters API (non-human by default). Voice consistency is only solved where a voice can be bound (Kling elements, Grok voices, Wan/Seedance/MiniMax audio references).

### Cited Findings
- Sora 2: "Keep descriptions consistent across shots, reuse phrasing for continuity, and avoid mixing traits that may compete." — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)

- Veo 3.1 ingredients: "Provide reference images of a scene, character, object, or style to maintain a consistent aesthetic across multiple shots. This feature now includes audio generation." - Omni Flash tags: "<IMAGE_REF_N> : use the image as a reference… <VIDEO_REF_N> : use the video as a character or object reference"; "Video references work best with likenesses; any audio in a video reference is ignored. - Wan reference-to-video: "The model preserves appearance, motion, voice timbre (if present), and background"; English format "Image 1", "Video 1"; Wan 2.6 uses "character1"… "Reference up to three characters at the same time" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)
- MiniMax H3 reference entry: "Images: ≤ 9… Videos: ≤ 3 clips; per-clip duration [2, 15] s… Audio: ≤ 3 clips… Mixed input is capped at 12 files in total" — [MiniMax video generation guide](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/video-generation)
- LTX-2.3: training "for motion, style or likeness (sound+appearance) can take less than an hour in many settings" (LoRA) — [Hugging Face LTX-2.3 model card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.3)
- fal (Kling): "define your core subjects clearly at the beginning of the prompt and keep descriptions consistent across shots" — [fal Kling 3.0 guide](https://blog.fal.ai/kling-3-0-prompting-guide)

### Inferences
- For a recurring French cast: keep a frozen "character bible" text block (age, face shape, hair, outfit, palette, voice timbre/accent) pasted verbatim into every prompt, and pair it with the model's reference feature; bind a voice wherever possible (Kling element voice, Grok voice reference, Wan/Seedance/MiniMax audio reference) because text voice descriptions alone drift between clips.
- For children's content in the EU, Omni Flash's ban on uploading images containing minors and Veo's "allow_adult only" for image-based generation in the EU are material constraints for a kids' franchise.

### Gaps
- Google Flow's own help pages on ingredients / character consistency were not read.
- No official data on voice consistency across separate generations without a bound voice.

## 6. How to specify sound effects, ambience and music

### Takeaway
Every model treats audio as a separate, explicitly written layer: describe ambience, individual SFX tied to on-screen actions, and either a named music style or an explicit "no music / no score". Defaults differ: Seedance 1.5 auto-adds background music, Wan auto-selects BGM unless told "No background music.", Omni Flash auto-generates a track, and LTX defaults to "flat studio sound" without audio direction.

### Cited Findings
- Veo: "Clearly specify if you want audio. We recommend that you use separate sentences in your prompt to describe the audio." Categories: "Sound effects… "the sound of a phone ringing"…", "Ambient noise… "the sounds of city traffic and distant sirens"…" — [Vertex AI video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide); label style "SFX: thunder cracks in the distance", "Ambient noise: the quiet hum of a starship bridge" — [Veo 3.1 prompting blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Omni Flash: "By default the model will try to generate an appropriate audio track… This is especially important if you want music in your video: Include calm background music / The video has a high energy techno beat / The audio is a low tinny radio broadcast in the background, playing a song" — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- Sora 2: "If your shot is silent, you can still suggest pacing with one small sound, such as "distant traffic hiss" or "a crisp snap."" and "The hum of espresso machines and the murmur of voices form the background." — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- Wan: "Sound effect = Source material + Action + Ambient sound. Example: A small glass ball falls from a table onto a wooden floor, making a "thud" sound in a quiet indoor environment." "Background music = Background music/score + Style." "To suppress background music, write 'No background music.'" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)
- Seedance 1.5: "By default, Seedance 1.5 Pro automatically generates background music that matches the prompt." Music example: "A cartoon character is in the center of the screen. The background music is a snippet of a fast-paced pop song. The requirement is for this cartoon character to clap hands in time with the drumbeats of the music." — [BytePlus Seedance-1.5-pro prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-1-5-pro)
- LTX-2.3: "Audio direction goes at the end of the prompt, after the dialogue and acting beats are laid out"; include "Acoustic space… Voice texture… Silence shape… Ambient cues"; "A prompt without any audio direction leaves the model to default to a flat studio sound." — [LTX blog, Directing Dialogue and Acting](https://ltx.io/blog/directing-dialogue-and-acting). LTX model card: "When generating audio without speech, the audio may be of lower quality." — [Hugging Face LTX-2.3 card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.3)
- LTX-2 sound vocabulary: "Setting: Ambient coffeeshop noises… Dialogue style: Energetic announcer, resonant voice with gravitas, distorted radio-style, robotic monotone, childlike curiosity. Volume: quiet whisper, mutters, shouts, screams" — [LTX blog prompting guide](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2)
- Kling 3.0: audio is a priced mode ("Native Audio" 12 credits/s at 1080p vs "No Native Audio" 8 credits/s) — [Kling VIDEO 3.0 Model User Guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide); fal examples interleave sound cues as short sentences ("Paper scraping sound.", "Music tightens with a rising pulse.") — [fal Kling 3.0 guide](https://blog.fal.ai/kling-3-0-prompting-guide)
- xAI: "Sound: a busy café under the dialogue throughout… never silent between lines." — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)

### Inferences
- Put "No music" / "No background music." explicitly for dialogue clips intended for TikTok, where music is added in the edit; otherwise Seedance/Wan/Omni may add BGM that clashes.

### Gaps
- No official loudness/mix controls except Sora's illustrative "-20 LUFS" phrasing (not a documented parameter).

## 7. Camera, lens and style keywords

### Takeaway
Official vocabularies overlap heavily: shot sizes (wide, medium, close-up, extreme close-up, over-the-shoulder, two-shot, POV), moves (static, pan, tilt, dolly, truck, pedestal, zoom, crane, drone, handheld, whip pan, arc/orbit), lens (wide-angle, telephoto, macro, shallow/deep DOF, rack focus, anamorphic, mm focal lengths, dolly zoom) and style anchors named early (film stock, era, animation style). One clear camera move per shot is the consistent rule.

### Cited Findings
- Veo camera movement list with examples, e.g. "Dolly (in/out)… "dolly out from the character to emphasize their isolation."", "Arc shot… "arc shot around a couple embracing in the rain."", "Vertigo effect (dolly zoom)…"; caveat "Some advanced camera angles are not officially supported." Style examples: "Pixar-like 3D animation", "claymation style", "stop-motion animation", "shot on 35mm film" — [Vertex AI video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide)
- Sora: "Each shot should have one clear camera move and one clear subject action. Actions work best when described in beats or counts"; weak "Actor walks across the room." vs strong "Actor takes four steps to the window, pauses, and pulls the curtain in the final second."; "Naming three to five colors helps keep the palette stable across shots." — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- LTX: "When describing camera movement, focus on the camera's relationship to the subject"; camera language list "follows, tracks, pans across, circles around, tilts upward, pushes in, pulls back, overhead view, handheld movement, over-the-shoulder, wide establishing shot, static frame"; LTX-2.3: "Avoid wide horizontal pans during dialogue" and "Hold the frame during pauses" — [LTX blog prompting guide](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2); [LTX dialogue blog](https://ltx.io/blog/directing-dialogue-and-acting)
- Seedance 1.5: "Using photographic terminology correctly can improve the quality of camera movements"; example "A medium shot from a flat position follows a skateboarding teenager, with a 45mm wide-angle lens level with his shoulder." and "Hitchcockian camera movement: Keep the girl's main composition unchanged, dolly out + increase the focal length of the lens." — [BytePlus Seedance-1.5-pro prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-1-5-pro)
- Wan: image-to-video camera wording "camera pushes in" or "camera moves left… To keep the camera still, use "fixed camera."" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)
- Kling 3.0 (partner guide): "describe how the camera behaves over time: tracking, following, freezing, panning… Long takes work particularly well when the camera's relationship to the subject is clearly defined" — [fal Kling 3.0 guide](https://blog.fal.ai/kling-3-0-prompting-guide); official example "The camera follows the professional woman steadily in a medium shot throughout… the camera tracks her as she walks and freezes instantly when she pauses" — [Kling VIDEO 3.0 guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide)
- MiniMax Hailuo 02: bracketed camera commands such as "[Pan left]", "[Push in]", "[Static shot]" — [Hedra Hailuo-02 Pro page](https://mkt.hedra.com/video-models/minimax-hailuo-02-pro); [minimax-ai.chat](https://minimax-ai.chat/?p=143) (both third party; official MiniMax doc not confirmed)

### Inferences
- For 9:16 TikTok dialogue, medium close-up / over-the-shoulder with a locked or slow push-in is the shot grammar most supported across vendors for clean lip-sync.

### Gaps
- Official MiniMax camera-command documentation for Hailuo 02 / H3 was not confirmed.

## 8. How negatives work (negative-prompt field vs "no subtitles" in text)

### Takeaway
Only some interfaces have a negative field: Vertex Veo documents negative prompts written as plain nouns (not "no"/"don't"), LTX's open pipeline uses a `negative_prompt`, and Wan/Akool examples mention negative audio prompts; Gemini Omni Flash has no negative parameter and officially says to write "Do not do X" / "No dialogue" inside the prompt; Wan officially uses in-text "No dialogue." / "No background music."; Sora and xAI examples use in-text constraints ("No added score", "nothing appears, disappears"). Negatives are reported to be weaker than positive descriptions (Veo subtitles case).

### Cited Findings
- Veo (Vertex): "Not recommended: using instructive language or words such as "no" or "don't". For example, avoid prompts such as "no walls" or "don't show walls". Recommended: Describe what you don't want to see. For example, "wall, frame"…" Example negative prompt: "urban background, man-made structures, dark, stormy, or threatening atmosphere." — [Vertex AI video prompt guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/video-gen-prompt-guide)
- Veo 3.1 blog (slightly different advice, in-prompt positive phrasing): "specify "a desolate landscape with no buildings or roads" instead of "no man-made structures"" — [Veo 3.1 prompting blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Gemini API Veo parameter table (Oct 2026) lists prompt, image, lastFrame, referenceImages, video, aspectRatio, durationSeconds, personGeneration, resolution (and seed) — no negative-prompt parameter appears on that page — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)
- Omni Flash: "System instructions, temperature, top_p, stop sequences, and negative prompts are not supported (you can put your negatives in the regular prompt: e.g., "Do not do X")" — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- LTX-2 diffusers example negative prompt (verbatim): "shaky, glitchy, low quality, worst quality, deformed, distorted, disfigured, motion smear, motion artifacts, fused fingers, bad anatomy, weird hand, ugly, transition, static." — [Hugging Face LTX-2 model card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2)
- Wan in-text suppressors: "No dialogue.", "No background music.", "Generate single shot." — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt); third party: negative audio prompt "robotic voice", "audio glitches" — [Akool](https://akool.com/blog-posts/getting-started-with-wan-2-5) (third party)
- Subtitles in Veo 3 persisted "even when the prompts… explicitly ask for no captions or subtitles"; negative prompts "usually less effective than positive ones" (researcher quoted) — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/15/1120156/googles-generative-video-model-veo-3-has-a-subtitles-problem/)
- Seedance I2V example constraint phrased positively: "keeping the appearance, proportions, and materials of both the lipstick and the model accurate and consistent, with no unnecessary elements added" — [BytePlus Seedance-1.5-pro prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-1-5-pro)

### Inferences
- Where a negative field exists (Vertex Veo, LTX, some Wan hosts), put "subtitles, captions, on-screen text, watermark, extra speakers, narrator, background music" there as bare nouns; where it doesn't (Omni, Kling, Seedance, Grok), add one short closing sentence ("No subtitles or on-screen text. Only the two characters speak.") and state the positive alternative ("clean frame, no text overlays").

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0 or Grok 1.5 APIs expose a negative-prompt field.

## 9. JSON vs prose prompting — does JSON help?

### Takeaway
No official vendor guide recommends JSON prompts; all official examples are prose or labeled plain-text sections, and LTX explicitly asks for a single flowing paragraph. Evidence that JSON helps is anecdotal (practitioner blogs), with skeptics noting no measurable gain; I found no controlled test.

### Cited Findings
- Official formats: Sora's labeled template ("Cinematography:", "Actions:", "Dialogue:") — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide); Veo labels "SFX:", "Ambient noise:" and timestamps — [Veo 3.1 blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1); xAI "Sound:", "Continuity:", "Lighting:" — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); LTX "Keep your prompt in a single flowing paragraph" — [LTX blog](https://ltx.io/blog/prompting-guide-for-ltx-2)
- Wan suggests generating prompts by passing the formula as a system message to an LLM (qwen3.6-plus), output being prose — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt); Veo blog suggests "use Gemini to analyze and enrich a simple prompt" — [Veo 3.1 blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Pro-JSON practitioner claims ("a shot list for AI video that helps you maintain consistency across multiple clips") — [Artlist blog](https://artlist.io/blog/veo-3-json-prompts/) (third party); skeptical view that "it doesn't matter much at all, because Veo often does whatever it wants anyway" — [The Creative Possible (Substack)](https://creativepossible.substack.com/p/should-you-prompt-in-json) (third party)
- LTX-2.3 warns its new text connector "doesn't ignore the noise" and reads structure literally — [LTX dialogue blog](https://ltx.io/blog/directing-dialogue-and-acting) (implies stray formatting tokens could matter; not specifically about JSON)

### Inferences
- Use JSON (or YAML) only as an internal authoring format in the pipeline, then render to labeled prose before sending; this keeps the benefits (fixed fields, reusable character block) without relying on an undocumented behaviour.

### Gaps
- No controlled A/B study of JSON vs prose for any model found.

## 10. How image-to-video prompts should differ from text-to-video prompts

### Takeaway
Officially, the image fixes subject, layout and style; the I2V prompt should describe what happens next — motion, camera, sound and speech — rather than re-describing the image (Wan states this explicitly; Sora, xAI, Kling and Seedance phrase it as "the prompt describes what happens next"). Veo's own I2V example does restate the scene briefly before adding motion, so a short restatement is tolerated, but contradictions with the image should be avoided.

### Cited Findings
- Wan: "The image defines the entity, scene, and style. Your prompt should focus on motion and camera movements. Prompt = Motion + Camera movement" — [Alibaba Cloud Model Studio prompt guide](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/text-to-video-prompt)
- Sora: "The model uses the image as an anchor for the first frame, while your text prompt defines what happens next." Examples: "She turns around and smiles, then slowly walks out of the frame." / "The fridge door opens. A cute, chubby purple monster comes out of it." Image "must match the target video's resolution (size)" — [Sora 2 Prompting Guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide)
- xAI: "The still you pass as image becomes the first frame, and the prompt describes what happens next. The output always matches the still's aspect ratio" — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview); earlier guide example: "Make the water crash down and slowly pan out the camera" — [xAI video generation guide](https://docs.x.ai/docs/guides/video-generations); official product I2V prompt: "Hero product reveal. A slow, smooth push-in toward the perfume bottle, ending a little closer with the label sharp and centered. Dappled sunlight drifts slowly across the scene… Only the camera moves." — [xAI Video Overview](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/overview)
- Kling (partner guide): "treat the input image as an anchor… Prompts should focus on how the scene evolves from the image: subtle movements, camera motion, or environmental changes." — [fal Kling 3.0 guide](https://blog.fal.ai/kling-3-0-prompting-guide); official Kling I2V example: "The camera gradually moves around to the front of the girl, who then lifts her head and smiles warmly at the camera, as if seeing an old friend after many years." — [Kling VIDEO 3.0 guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide)
- Seedance 1.5 I2V examples: "Based on this image, generate a video that shows the rain outside the window getting slightly heavier, raindrops merging into streams and flowing down the glass, and a pedestrian with an umbrella hurrying past outside the window."; lipstick ad: "Generate a video based on the input lipstick product keyframe image, keeping the appearance, proportions, and materials of both the lipstick and the model accurate and consistent, with no unnecessary elements added… A clear, confident female commercial voice-over with a refined tone and moderate speaking pace is synchronized with the visuals, delivering the following lines: "Rich color. Smooth texture. One swipe delivers radiant lips. Lightweight, comfortable, and effortlessly elegant."" — [BytePlus Seedance-1.5-pro prompt guide](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/ModelArk/seedance-1-5-pro)
- Veo I2V official example re-describes the scene then adds motion: "A surreal, cinematic macro video. Tiny surfers ride perpetual, rolling waves inside a stone bathroom sink. A running vintage brass faucet generates the endless surf. The camera slowly pans across the whimsical, sunlit scene as the miniature figures expertly carve the turquoise water." ("Veo uses the input image as the initial frame.") — [Gemini API Veo page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo); Veo first+last frame prompt describes only the transition and the song line: "The camera performs a smooth 180-degree arc shot, starting with the front-facing view of the singer and circling around her to seamlessly end on the POV shot from behind her on stage. The singer sings "when you look me in the eyes, I can see a million stars."" — [Veo 3.1 blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Omni Flash: "For best results with image-to-video, use high-resolution images and provide specific motion descriptions. Vague prompts like "make it move" produce less compelling results"; the model "will decide how to use the image" unless tagged <FIRST_FRAME> or told "Use this image as the starting frame." — [Gemini API Omni page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)
- Kling 3.0 element binding is recommended on top of a start frame to keep the subject stable through camera moves ("Start Frame + Enhanced Element Consistency Output" vs "Only Start Frame Output") — [Kling VIDEO 3.0 guide](https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide)
- LTX-2.3 is published with pipeline tag image-to-video; frame count must be "divisible by 8 + 1" and width/height divisible by 32 — [Hugging Face LTX-2.3 card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.3)

### Inferences
- I2V maxi prompt = (optional one-line identity reminder of who is in the frame, e.g. "The grandmother on the left, the boy on the right") + timed motion beats + speech lines with speaker anchors + camera + audio + constraints. Restating outfit/colour details that already show in the image adds tokens without benefit and risks contradiction; naming who is who is still useful for lip-sync attribution.

### Gaps
- No official measurement of whether restating image content helps or hurts.
